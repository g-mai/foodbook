## Instructions

1. Trim away any tough gristle, leaving the softer connective tissue and fat. Cut the beef into even pieces about 3 cm across.
2. Finely chop the garlic and crack the peppercorns coarsely. Warm the olive oil in a heavy pot over medium-low heat. Briefly cook the garlic and pepper until fragrant, taking care not to brown the garlic.
3. Add the rosemary, bay leaves, and beef. Brown the meat on all sides for 5–6 minutes, working in batches if the pot is crowded.
4. Pour in half of the room-temperature wine and let it simmer for a couple of minutes. Stir the tomato paste into the hot stock, then add it to the pot along with the remaining wine. Season lightly with salt.
5. Cover and cook at the lowest heat for about 4 hours. Keep the liquid at a very gentle simmer and check every 40 minutes or so; if the beef is no longer covered, add a little hot water.
6. Uncover for the final 30 minutes so the sauce can reduce. The beef is ready when a fork slips in easily and the pieces yield when pressed. Taste, adjust the salt, and serve hot with the sauce.

## Notes

- Choose a collagen-rich cut such as shin, chuck, or cheek. Lean cuts can dry out during the long braise.
- The cooking time can vary by cut; if the meat is still firm after 4 hours, keep it covered and simmer until tender, checking every 15 minutes.
- The flavor improves overnight. Chill leftovers promptly and reheat gently with a splash of water or stock.
- Serve with unsalted Tuscan bread, polenta, cannellini beans, or mashed potatoes.
